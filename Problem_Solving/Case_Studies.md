# 🌍 Real-World Case Studies for Problem Solving Analysis

## Case Study 1: Coca-Cola “New Coke” (1985)

Coca-Cola reformulated its flagship product after taste tests showed a majority preferred the new version over Pepsi. After launch, backlash was immediate and severe.

### Key Question

Why did a data-driven decision fail?

### Analysis

* Blind taste tests ignored **brand loyalty and emotional attachment**
* Tests measured *sip preference*, not *full-consumption behavior*
* Cultural identity tied to original formula was underestimated

✅ Lesson: **Good data + wrong context = bad decisions**

***

## Case Study 2: Netflix Qwikster Split (2011)

Netflix announced it would split DVD rentals and streaming into separate services, raising prices. Customers reacted negatively and subscriptions dropped.

### Key Question

What did Netflix misunderstand?

### Analysis

* Underestimated importance of **simplicity for users**
* Ignored customer perception of value
* Failed to test messaging and rollout strategy

✅ Lesson: **Operational logic ≠ customer logic**

***

## Case Study 3: Wells Fargo Sales Scandal (2016)

Employees opened millions of unauthorized accounts to meet aggressive sales targets.

### Key Question

How did a performance system create fraud?

### Analysis

* Incentives rewarded quantity, not quality
* Unrealistic targets pressured employees
* Leadership ignored warning signals

✅ Lesson: **Metrics drive behavior—sometimes the wrong behavior**

***

## Case Study 4: Boeing 737 MAX Crisis (2018–2019)

A software system (MCAS) caused crashes linked to design and training failures.

### Key Question

Where did the system breakdown occur?

### Analysis

* Over-reliance on automation
* Lack of pilot training on new system
* Poor risk communication

✅ Lesson: **Complex systems fail at the intersection of design, training, and communication**

***

## Case Study 5: Target Data Breach (2013)

Hackers accessed millions of customer records via a third-party vendor.

### Key Question

What vulnerability was overlooked?

### Analysis

* Third-party security not adequately monitored
* Alerts were ignored
* System complexity created blind spots

✅ Lesson: **Security is only as strong as the weakest link**

***

## Case Study 6: Uber Surge Pricing Backlash

During emergencies (e.g., storms), Uber prices surged dramatically, causing public outrage.

### Key Question

Why did a rational pricing model fail?

### Analysis

* Algorithm lacked **ethical/contextual awareness**
* Customers perceived exploitation
* No safeguards for extreme situations

✅ Lesson: **Optimization without ethics can damage trust**

***

## Case Study 7: Blockbuster vs Netflix

Blockbuster had opportunities to acquire Netflix but declined, focusing on retail dominance.

### Key Question

Why did Blockbuster fail?

### Analysis

* Ignored digital disruption trends
* Overvalued existing business model
* Misread customer behavior shifts

✅ Lesson: **Success can blind organizations to future risk**

***

## Case Study 8: Google Flu Trends Failure

Google attempted to predict flu outbreaks using search data but significantly overestimated cases.

### Key Question

Why did big data fail here?

### Analysis

* Algorithm overfit historical patterns
* Didn’t adjust for behavior changes (media influence)
* Lacked ground-truth validation

✅ Lesson: **Big data ≠ accurate data**

***

## Case Study 9: JCPenney Pricing Strategy (2012)

JCPenney eliminated sales and coupons, switching to “everyday low pricing.” Sales dropped sharply.

### Key Question

Why did customers reject lower prices?

### Analysis

* Customers enjoyed the *feeling* of discounts
* Misunderstood behavioral psychology
* Failed change management

✅ Lesson: **Perception of value often outweighs actual value**

***

## Case Study 10: Volkswagen Emissions Scandal (2015)

Volkswagen installed software to cheat emissions tests.

### Key Question

What led to systemic ethical failure?

### Analysis

* Pressure to meet impossible standards
* Culture discouraged dissent
* Short-term goals prioritized over compliance

✅ Lesson: **Organizational culture shapes ethical outcomes**

***

## Case Study 11: Theranos Collapse

Theranos claimed revolutionary blood-testing technology that ultimately failed validation.

### Key Question

How did investors and partners miss the warning signs?

### Analysis

* Lack of transparency
* Overreliance on founder narrative
* Weak technical due diligence

✅ Lesson: **Trust without verification is risky**

***

## Case Study 12: Amazon “Fire Phone” Failure

Amazon launched a smartphone that failed to gain traction.

### Key Question

Why did Amazon misjudge this market?

### Analysis

* Entered saturated market late
* Product lacked compelling differentiation
* Overestimated ecosystem advantage

✅ Lesson: **Capabilities in one domain don’t guarantee success in another**

***

# 🧭 How to Use These in Class

Have students:

1. **Identify missing data**
2. **List assumptions**
3. **Define the real problem (not the obvious one)**
4. **Propose alternative explanations**
5. **Suggest better decision frameworks**

***

# ⚠️ Cross-Case Pitfalls Highlighted

These real-world examples reinforce:

* **Overconfidence in data** (Google Flu Trends, New Coke)
* **Ignoring human behavior** (JCPenney, Uber)
* **Metric misalignment** (Wells Fargo)
* **Failure to question assumptions** (Blockbuster)
* **Ethical blind spots** (Volkswagen, Theranos)
* **System complexity risks** (Boeing, Target)
