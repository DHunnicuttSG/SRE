# 🧠 Problem Set: Ambiguous Analytical Challenges

## Problem 1

A company reports a 20% increase in performance after implementing a new system.  
Was the system effective?

***

## Problem 2

A class of 30 students has an average score of 80. One student drops the course. The new average becomes 82.  
What can you infer?

***

## Problem 3

Two departments report identical revenue this year, but one is declared “more efficient.”  
Why might that be true?

***

## Problem 4

A survey says 70% of people prefer Product A over Product B.  
Should the company stop producing Product B?

***

## Problem 5

A graph shows a strong upward trend in sales over 6 months. At month 7, sales drop sharply.  
What explains the drop?

***

## Problem 6

A hospital reduces patient wait time by 50%, but complaints increase.  
What might be happening?

***

## Problem 7

A coin is flipped 10 times and lands heads 8 times.  
Is the coin biased?

***

## Problem 8

A project is 90% complete but has used 95% of its budget.  
Is the project successful?

***

## Problem 9

Two investment options both return 10% annually. One is more volatile.  
Which is better?

***

## Problem 10

An employee claims productivity doubled after introducing a new workflow.  
What additional data would you need?

***

## Problem 11

You are told a model is 95% accurate.  
Is it a good model?

***

## Problem 12

A factory increased output by 15% but profits declined.  
Explain possible causes.

***







# ✅ Problem Analysis

These are not “one right answer” problems, the focus is on reasoning.

***

### Problem 1 Analysis

* Missing baseline: 20% of what?
* Was there a control group?
* Were external factors responsible (seasonality, staffing)?
  ✅ Insight: **Correlation ≠ causation**

***

### Problem 2 Analysis

* Original total = 30 × 80 = 2400
* New total = 29 × 82 = 2378
* Dropped student scored: 2400 − 2378 = 22  
  ✅ Insight: Outliers significantly affect averages

***

### Problem 3 Analysis

Possible reasons:

* Lower costs
* Fewer employees
* Better resource utilization  
  ✅ Efficiency ≠ output alone

***

### Problem 4 Analysis

* Sample size unclear
* Survey bias possible
* Product B may serve niche customers
  ✅ Avoid decisions on incomplete data

***

### Problem 5 Analysis

Possible explanations:

* Seasonal demand ended
* Data anomaly
* External shock (economy, competition)  
  ✅ Trend breaks require context

***

### Problem 6 Analysis

* Faster service may feel rushed
* Quality may have decreased
* Expectations increased  
  ✅ Optimizing one metric may harm others

***

### Problem 7 Analysis

* Small sample size
* Use hypothesis testing  
  ✅ Not enough evidence to conclude bias

***

### Problem 8 Analysis

* Budget inefficiency
* Risk of overrun
* Estimate quality questionable  
  ✅ Progress % ≠ cost efficiency

***

### Problem 9 Analysis

Depends on:

* Risk tolerance
* Time horizon
* Variance/standard deviation  
  ✅ Same mean ≠ same value

***

### Problem 10 Analysis

Need:

* Definition of productivity
* Time period
* External factors
* Measurement method  
  ✅ Claims require operational definitions

***

### Problem 11 Analysis

Ask:

* Accuracy relative to what?
* Class imbalance?
* Precision/recall?  
  ✅ Accuracy can be misleading

***

### Problem 12 Analysis

* Increased costs (labor/materials)
* Lower margins
* Discounts or overproduction  
  ✅ Output gains don’t guarantee profit

***

# ⚠️ Common Pitfalls & Mistakes

## 1. Assuming Missing Information

* Students jump to conclusions without asking clarifying questions
* **Fix:** Identify unknowns first

***

## 2. Confusing Correlation with Causation

* “X increased after Y → Y caused X”
* **Fix:** Look for controls, confounders

***

## 3. Ignoring Sample Size

* Small samples → unreliable conclusions
* **Fix:** Always ask “how much data?”

***

## 4. Overreliance on Averages

* Averages hide variability and outliers
* **Fix:** Consider distribution, median, variance

***

## 5. Not Defining Terms

* Words like “efficient,” “better,” “productive” are vague
* **Fix:** Demand measurable definitions

***

## 6. Neglecting Context

* Time, environment, incentives often missing
* **Fix:** Ask “what else changed?”

***

## 7. Data Interpretation Errors

* Misreading graphs (axes, scaling)
* **Fix:** Check units, scale, and timeframes

***

## 8. Single-Metric Thinking

* Optimizing one variable harms others
* **Fix:** Think in systems and trade-offs

***

## 9. Bias Blindness

* Survey bias, confirmation bias, survivorship bias
* **Fix:** Question source and methodology

***

## 10. Ignoring Risk

* Same averages ≠ same outcomes
* **Fix:** Evaluate variance and uncertainty

***

## 11. Premature Conclusions

* Answering before fully analyzing
* **Fix:** Slow down—structure thinking first

***

# 🧭 Teaching Strategy Tip

Encourage students to always ask:

* **What assumptions are being made?**
* **What data is missing?**
* **What alternative explanations exist?**
* **What would disprove this conclusion?**
